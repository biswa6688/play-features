AI Rating Scoring

How the total score is calculated

A recording's rating total score is a weighted roll-up of every answered question — category weight × question weight × answer weight — with N/A answers excluded and everything else renormalized to fill the gap they leave.

Recording Detail→AI Ratings panel→Total Score

01

What "total score" means

One number, built from many small ones.

Every rated recording is scored against a process version: a set of categories (Opening, Tone, Resolution, Closing…), each holding a handful of questions ("Did the agent greet the customer?"). A reviewer — or the AI — answers each question from a fixed option list (e.g. Good Average Poor N/A), and each option carries its own percentage.

The totalScore stored on the rating is the weighted average of every answered question, expressed as a single percentage out of 100.

02

The three ingredients

Every question's contribution comes from multiplying three independent weights together.

Category weightage How much this category is worth out of the whole assessment. All categories on a process version sum to 100%.
Question weightage How much this question is worth inside its own category. A category's own questions sum to 100% — this number is never compared across categories.
Answer weightage What the chosen answer is worth — a percentage configured per answer option, e.g. Good = 100%, Average = 60%, Poor = 30%.

The critical thing to hold onto: question weightage is a percentage of its category, not of the whole assessment. A question's 25% means something different depending on whether its category is worth 15% or 25% of the total — keep the two weight levels separate until the final multiply.

03

Step 1 — score a single question

The per-question formula, with no N/A in the picture yet.

For one answered question:

Per-question contribution contribution = (questionWeightage × answerWeightage ÷ 100) × (categoryWeightage ÷ 100)

questionWeightage is % of its category · categoryWeightage is % of the whole assessment · answerWeightage is the % configured for the chosen option.

Example — a question worth 25% of a category worth 15% of the assessment, answered Good (100%):

(25 × 100 ÷ 100) × (15 ÷ 100) = 25 × 0.15 = 3.75

That single question adds 3.75 percentage points to the recording's total score.

04

Step 2 — roll a category up, then sum every category

The total score is just this contribution, summed over every answered question, everywhere.

Total score totalScore = Σ over all answered questions of contribution

If a process version has 6 categories of 4 questions each, and every single question is answered (no N/A anywhere), this sum naturally lands on 100% when every answer is Good — because the category weights already sum to 100%, and each category's question weights already sum to 100%. The moment any question comes back N/A, that stops being true unless something redistributes the weight it leaves behind — which is Step 3.

05

Step 3 — handling N/A answers

The part that's easy to get wrong when reading a rating by hand.

A question answered N/A contributes nothing — but its weight can't just vanish, or the remaining weights no longer sum to 100% and the total score deflates. The weight has to be redistributed to the questions and categories that still have an answer. That redistribution happens at two independent levels, matching the two independent weight scopes from Step 1:

A — within the category If some (not all) of a category's questions are N/A, the category's own question weights are renormalized across only its answered questions, so they still sum to 100% of that category.
B — across categories If every question in a category is N/A, the whole category drops out, and category weightages are renormalized across only the categories that still have at least one answered question, so they still sum to 100%.
Why two separate levels

Question weightage only ever means something within its own category, and category weightage only ever means something across the whole assessment. Redistributing N/A weight has to respect that same boundary — level A stays inside one category, level B moves between categories — otherwise a question's weight would leak into categories it was never scoped to.

Micro-example — level A only

One category, worth 15% of the assessment, with 4 equally-weighted questions (25% each). Three are answered Good; the fourth comes back N/A.

The category's weight (15%) is untouched — only the 25% that Q4 would have carried is redistributed across Q1–Q3.

Renormalizing Q1–Q3 25 ÷ 75 × 100 = 33.33% each

75 is the combined original weight of the three still-answered questions (25 + 25 + 25) — Q4's 25% is folded back in across them.

The category still scores out of a full 100% internally; it's just resting on 3 questions instead of 4.

07

Full worked example

6 categories, 4 questions each, weighted 15 / 15 / 15 / 15 / 15 / 25 — two categories fully N/A.

Category 3 and Category 4 come back completely N/A (every question) — that's level B: they drop out entirely, and the remaining four categories' weights are renormalized to fill the 30% they leave behind. Category 5 and 6 have every question answered, but with a mix of options, so Good (100%), Average (60%), and Poor (30%) all appear.

Category Orig. Cat Wgt Adj. Cat Wgt Question Adj. Q Wgt Answer Ans Wgt Contribution
Total score —
Renormalizing category weights (Cat 1, 2, 5, 6 survive) 15 + 15 + 15 + 25 = 70 ← combined original weight of the surviving categories Cat 1, 2, 5: 15 ÷ 70 × 100 = 21.43% Cat 6: 25 ÷ 70 × 100 = 35.71%

Cat 3 and Cat 4's combined 30% is gone from the denominator entirely — it never gets added anywhere, it's simply absent once the remaining weights are redivided.

What changed for Category 5 & 6's own answers Cat 5 = (100 + 100 + 60 + 30) ÷ 4 = 72.5% of its own weight Cat 6 = (30 + 100 + 30 + 60) ÷ 4 = 55% of its own weight

No N/A in either category, so their question weights stay at 25% each — only the category weight above changes.
